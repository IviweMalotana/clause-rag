# Know Your Customer (KYC) Onboarding Procedure

**Northwind Pay, Inc. — Synthetic sample document for demonstration purposes only.**
Process owner: Head of Onboarding Operations. Version 3.0. Effective 1 March 2026.

## 1. Overview

This procedure describes how Northwind Pay verifies the identity of new customers
before granting access to financial products. It operationalizes the firm's AML
Policy at the point of onboarding and defines the evidence required to establish
who a customer is and the purpose of the relationship.

## 2. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

Standard Customer Due Diligence is required for every new customer. CDD consists of
three steps: (a) collect identifying information, (b) verify that information
against an independent and reliable source, and (c) assess the intended nature and
purpose of the relationship. An account may not transact until CDD is complete and
the customer's risk rating has been assigned.

## 3. Identity Evidence — Individuals

For individual customers, Northwind Pay collects full legal name, date of birth,
residential address, and a government-issued identifier. Identity is verified using
at least one government-issued photo document (passport, national ID, or driving
licence) plus one proof of address dated within the last 90 days. Document
authenticity is checked using automated document verification and a liveness selfie
match.

## 4. Identity Evidence — Businesses

For business customers, Northwind Pay collects the registered legal name,
registration number, registered address, and the nature of the business. The firm
identifies and verifies every beneficial owner who holds 25% or more of the entity,
as well as the individuals who exercise control. A business account may not be
approved until at least one authorized signatory has completed individual CDD.

## 5. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

Enhanced Due Diligence is mandatory for any customer rated High risk, including all
politically exposed persons (PEPs), their family members, and known close
associates. EDD requires senior management approval to open the account,
establishment of the customer's source of funds and source of wealth, and more
frequent ongoing review. A PEP relationship may not be approved by onboarding staff
alone; it must be approved by a Compliance Officer.

## 6. Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)

A PEP is an individual entrusted with a prominent public function. All customers are
screened for PEP status at onboarding and on an ongoing basis. A positive PEP screen
does not automatically result in refusal, but it always triggers the EDD process in
Section 5 and a documented source-of-funds assessment.

## 7. Ongoing Monitoring and Review

Customer information is kept current through periodic review. Low and Medium risk
customers are reviewed at least every 24 months; High risk customers are reviewed at
least every 6 months. A review is also triggered by a material change, such as a
change of beneficial ownership, a sanctions or PEP hit, or a transaction monitoring
alert that indicates the customer profile is no longer accurate.

## 8. Failure to Verify

If a customer cannot be satisfactorily identified and verified, the account must not
be opened, or if already opened on a conditional basis, it must be suspended and
escalated to Compliance. Onboarding staff must not override a failed verification.
The reason for any refusal is documented and retained.
